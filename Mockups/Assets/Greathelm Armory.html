Armor

Every armor rolls 1d6 on the armor table when its wearer is dealt damage. What changes is how many faces of the die pierce, and how much speed you get back for wearing less.

Choosing and building your models

Mustering your six

The rules that decide which miniatures you can field and what has to be on them.

Six a side

A basic game uses 6 models each, from any 28–32 mm range, on 25 mm round bases. There are no points values.

What you see is what you get

The weapons on the miniature are the weapons it uses in the game.

One loadout each

A set of armor, then either a one-handed weapon, a shield and an item, or a two-handed weapon and an item.

Two weapons

Two of the same weapon give +1 to parry defense; their abilities do not stack. With two different weapons, choose one each time you attack or defend. At most 2 dual-wielding models unless your opponent agrees to more.

One weapon, empty hand

A knight with a single one-handed weapon and no shield gets +1 to the clash test on bash actions. Soldiers do not get this.

Full plate in quick play

If both players agree, any heavily armored knight may count as having the full plate upgrade.

Soldiers

Up to 4 of your 6 may be soldiers. They follow the knight rules with fixed gear, a lower damage limit than a knight’s 3, and one bonus each.
